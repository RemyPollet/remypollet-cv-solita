How I work

Five things you can hold me to.

Customer pain → architecture

Every architecture decision I make has a customer ticket behind it. "What problem does this solve?" is the only meaningful refactor question.

Boring infra > clever infra

Postgres, Tailwind, server-side rendering, single-region until proven otherwise. I reach for "exotic" only when the boring path is measurably broken.

Ship + own + support

I won't design something I'd hate to be paged for. The Head of Helpdesk role keeps that honest — I get the call when my architecture fails.

AI as a collaborator, not a magic wand

I pair with Claude daily. Not because it's trendy — because the right LLM in the loop turns "this would take a sprint" into "this can ship today." This page is partly proof.

Calm under escalation

A panicked customer doesn't need a panicked engineer. The job is to translate "everything is on fire" into "here's what we know, here's what we're doing, here's the next update." Then keep that promise. Track record of doing this without losing my Tuesday.

Outside the laptop

What I do when I'm not shipping.

I live in Bruges, which is why several of my products end up Bruges-themed (PostYourCard's hotel partners are local, the survey's example tenant is Brugge). Working from a tourist city teaches you more about user-onboarding edge cases than you'd think.

I run my own chess club in Bruges — weekly evenings, mixed levels, a small community I built and still organise. It's the same loop as my products, just with pawns instead of Postgres: notice a gap, build the thing, keep it running.

On weekends I make music and perform live — with my brother and my uncle, both of whom also happen to be partners on the side businesses. Stage on Saturday, sprint review on Monday. The people I keep building things with are family, by accident.

I'm a multi-vertical entrepreneur by accident: I helped my nonkel launch his postcard business and ended up building him a SaaS. I helped a partner with cow-tracking and ended up building a vertical. The pattern is always the same: this is too painful by hand, let me build the boring tool.

Honestly: I rarely keep a 40-hour work week. I don't count my weeks in hours — I count them in interests and ideas I picked up, learned, or tried to build. Sometimes that's a schema migration, sometimes a chess opening, sometimes a chord change. All equally welcome.

— or testing a new tag prototype on a fridge in my kitchen.

What I'm looking for

Why this role, this team, this year.

I've spent the last three years being the only architect in the room. I want to be the only architect in my room — surrounded by 400 colleagues who've solved the problem already, and one customer who needs it solved again, differently.

The Solita description (especially the "holistic approach … sustainable, production-ready AI solutions" part) maps to how I already work, just at a smaller scale. The growth I want is upward depth — bigger customers, more architectural rigor, real consulting craft — not sideways breadth.

And honestly: I want to spend more days in customer workshops sketching architectures, and fewer days running vercel deploy --prod at 22:00 because the auto-deploy hook broke.
