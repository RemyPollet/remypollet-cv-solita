D · The bridge

Head of Helpdesk who codes the fix.

My title at Blyott (current job) is Head of Helpdesk. In practice, I'm the person who picks up the customer ticket — and the person who writes the migration that fixes it.

Most architects design and hand off. Most support leads translate but don't ship. I do both, because Blyott is small enough that I have to. That's exactly the muscle Solita's role description asks for: "translate customer needs into clear, credible, and scalable architectures."

Real example from this week: a hospital ICU was getting duplicate alerts for the same out-of-range fridge. The customer escalated to me. I read the alert engine, found the missing dedup window, designed the fix (per-config 1-hour throttle, 5-min for critical), shipped it to production, and updated the customer on what changed. Same person, same day. That's not a job description — that's just Tuesday.

And that is exactly why Solita pulls me.

Today I'm the only architect, the helpdesk lead, the sales engineer, and the one who reboots the gateway at 03:00. It works — but it doesn't scale, and the architecture half stays shallower than I want it to be.

At Solita I'd be the same customer-facing architect, but with 400 colleagues behind me who've already solved the problem.

Less noise. More depth.
Whiteboard at 14:00, no pager at 03:00.

That's the difference between surviving as a founder and growing as an architect. I'm picking the second.
